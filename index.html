The Vision

Not a factory.
A neighborhood asset that happens to make things.

7100 S Cottage Grove is a half-acre triangular corner lot on Chicago's South Side. Today it sits empty. The plan is to transform it into a working campus that builds things — homes, furniture, art, food — and puts those things back into the community that surrounds it.

The MAX CNC Innovation Campus is a mixed-use production and community facility anchored by a prefabricated housing workshop. It combines artisanal manufacturing, a design gallery, an urban greenhouse, and a café into a single site.

Why Prefab

90 years of disinvestment. A new way to build back.

The disparities visible in Chicago's housing market today are the result of decades of structured exclusion and uneven access to capital. The disinvestment on South and West Sides still tells the story — unequal wealth distribution, unequal ownership. The question driving this project: how can we create scalable, adaptable housing in Black communities?

  • Regular home construction averages $162/sf — prohibitively expensive at scale
  • Manufactured prefab homes average $84/sf — nearly half the cost
  • Cut construction time by up to 60%, reduce waste by 30–40%
  • Build year-round in a controlled factory environment regardless of weather
  • Feeds directly into City Lots for Working Families ($1 lot program)

The Housing Product

Wood + SIP hybrid. Volumetric assembly. Design-forward homes.

After evaluating concrete, steel, CLT, and ICF systems, the project selected a wood framing and Structural Insulated Panel (SIP) hybrid — the best combination of lower cost, faster construction, scalability, and transport compatibility for Chicago's South and West Side context.

Assembly System

Volumetric prefabrication — complete room modules built in the workshop, transported to site, and craned into place. Minimal on-site labor, fast mass deployment, and the most cost-effective approach for the target communities.

Unit Design

Double-stacked units (Tier 1 preferred): ground floor with living area and bedroom, first floor with studio/flex space, second bedroom, and balcony. Solar analysis confirms optimal orientation for year-round daylighting.

Material Logic

SIPs deliver excellent thermal performance and fast construction speed. Wood framing is renewable, low-cost, and familiar to local trades. The hybrid system keeps per-unit costs at manufactured-home levels while delivering architect-designed quality.

Norman Teague & Titobi

Design rooted in community

Norman Teague (MFA, School of the Art Institute of Chicago) is a Professor of Industrial Design at UIC and a design/build practitioner whose work is in the permanent collections of MoMA, LACMA, the Smithsonian National Museum of African American History and Culture, and the Art Institute of Chicago. He served as a consultant on the Obama Presidential Center design team and co-founded the Design Apprenticeship Program at the University of Chicago Arts Incubator. Through Titobi — co-founded with architect Max Davis — the gallery showcases furniture and objects produced on-site alongside rotating exhibitions from South and West Side artists.

Community Anchor

A gathering place for the South Side

The courtyard and campus host community events, outdoor exhibitions, pop-up markets, and informal gathering — activating a corner that has sat vacant for years. Solar panels on both Quonset roofs reduce operating costs and qualify for Chicago PACE financing and federal tax credits.

Workforce Pipeline

From classroom to fabrication floor

Through a partnership with FutureGrow and Chicago Public Schools, the campus connects CPS students to a professional design and fabrication studio ecosystem — with paid internships, team-based challenges, and a pathway toward a formal CTE endorsement in Design, Fabrication, and Community Media.

Already Proven

In 2024, high school students worked with Tim Nicholson of UChicago to design original sculptures using 3D modeling software. Designs were 3D printed at the studio. Max Davis and the team taught metal casting. Students poured their own work in metal. The finished sculptures were exhibited publicly at the National Museum of Mexican Art. This model is directly replicable through CPS co-op and capstone placements.

The Entrepreneurial Track

Students who learn 3D design and fabrication can immediately produce original products: collectible figures, functional objects, limited-edition art pieces. They are not just building skills — they are creating product lines with real market value. The program integrates design, fabrication, marketing, and sales into a single pipeline connecting to CPS Business and Entrepreneurship CTE programming.
